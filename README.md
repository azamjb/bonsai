Bonsai

An iOS app that reduces screen time through social accountability. Instead of self imposed limits that can easily be dismissed, Bonsai invokes social accountability to help you stick to your goals

The problem:

Every screen time app has the same flaw: the person enforcing the limit is the person trying to break it. Apple's built-in Screen Time, Freedom, Opal — all of them ship with a dismiss button, and the dismiss button always wins. Self-imposed limits fail because leaving the problem to willpower alone is what causes screen time addiction.

The approach:

Bonsai removes self-override entirely and replaces it with a second person.

When you join, you invite an Accountability Partner — a friend, partner, or family member willing to be involved. You then set limits on the apps you want restricted, enforced through Apple's Screen Time API.

When you hit a limit, you can request more time. That request sends an SMS to your partner containing a unique 6-digit code. Only they can give it to you, and whether they do is entirely their call.

If you genuinely need your phone and your partner isn't reachable or says no, a manual override removes all limits for the rest of the day — for a small fee.

Why it works

Two mechanisms, both deliberate.

Social friction. Asking someone for permission to keep scrolling is an admission that you're struggling with a goal you set for yourself. That moment of hesitation is the intervention. The American Society of Training and Development found that telling someone about a goal raises the chance of meeting it to 65%; Bonsai extends this by making the partner an active participant rather than an observer.

Financial friction. The paid override exists so there's always an exit — but one that costs something. A small charge is enough to make impulsive use feel like a decision rather than a reflex.
